## 1.1. Industrial conceptual apparatus (PBL Glossary)

The analysis and design of streaming architectures require a strict conceptual framework that excludes everyday interpretations and is based on music industry standards, regulatory documents and academic research.

* **DSP (Digital Service Provider (DSP)):** Common industry and legal term for music streaming platforms (Spotify, Apple Music, YouTube Music, Deezer, SoundCloud). A DSP is a centralized hardware and software infrastructure that licenses, indexes, hosts and streams a digital catalog of commercial and independent music to end users on a subscription (SVOD) or advertising-subsidized (AVOD) model.


* **(Music) Recommendation Algorithm:** A complex set of data-driven mathematical rules, heuristics and statistical models (collaborative filtering, matrix factorization, graph embeddings, audio content analysis) used by the DSP to predict the likelihood of a listener having a positive interaction with the next audio stream.


* **Black-Box Algorithm:** A system or computing process that takes user actions as input (listening history, skipping, adding to playlists) and produces a generated queue of tracks, completely hiding from the user the internal mechanics of decision-making, ranking criteria, feature weights and commercial priorities.


* **Seed Track:** The original composition selected by the user or session, whose metadata and acoustic parameters serve as the root mathematical vector (centroid) for initializing the recommendation algorithm.


* **Song Radio:** Automatic discovery tool that generates a continuous stream of songs based on the selected *Seed Track*. Tracks are selected based on the user's historical profile and source song metadata, running sequentially without direct listener intervention.


* **Autoplay:** A continuous streaming mechanism that is activated when an explicitly defined user context (album, playlist, queue, or individual track) ends. Technologically and algorithmically, it reproduces the logic of *Song Radio*, automatically filling the session with conditionally relevant content.


* **Smart Shuffle:** An algorithmic playlist playback mode in which the standard pseudo-random permutation generator is replaced by mixing third-party recommendations that match the hypothetical style of the current list.


* **Discovery Tools:** Specific DSP functionality designed to navigate through an unknown catalog. Includes algorithmic personal selections (Discover Weekly, Release Radar), contextual mixes (Daylist), search filters and generative interfaces based on text prompts (AI DJ, AI Playlists).


* **User (User / Listener):** The end consumer of audio content on the platform, serving as a source of behavioral telemetry (streams, skips, retention time) and monetization value (viewing advertising, paying for a subscription).


* **Creator:** The primary author and copyright holder of a musical work, uploading content to the platform directly or through aggregators, seeking to expand the audience and receive royalties.


* **From Owning to Renting:** A fundamental sociocultural shift in media consumption. The historical model, based on the purchase of physical media (vinyl, CD) or local digital files (MP3) with manual organization of a personal library, has been replaced by access to cloud rental of catalogs of 100+ million tracks. The consequence of this shift has been a loss of user autonomy and a critical dependence on the platform's opaque recommendation systems.


* **Stakeholder Matrix of the Music Ecosystem (Stakeholder Landscape):** Dynamic node of conflicting economic and operational interests of key market participants:



| Stakeholder | Core value and purpose | Barriers and conflicts of interest |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Listeners** | Quick search for resonating music, relevance to the current mood, queue control, transparency of recommendations. | Algorithmic semantic isolation, radio looping, aggressive paywall, imposed content. |
| **Creators** | Reaching the target audience, transparency of distribution algorithms, sustainable monetization and recognition. | Diktat of majors, micro-royalties ($0.003), the need to adjust creativity to retention metrics. |
| **Platforms (DSPs)** | Maximizing session time (Time Spent), increasing LTV, reducing churn (Churn), optimizing royalty payments. | Conflict: fast and accurate search reduces wandering around advertising; Indie promotion depends on major contracts. |
| **Rights Holders** | Maximizing market share (Market Share), priority catalog rotation, exclusive license guarantees. | They oppose the transfer of control to the user, blocking the promotion of an open and unlicensed indie catalogue. |
| **Metadata Services** | Structuring knowledge about music (MusicBrainz, Discogs, Last.fm). | Lack of deep acoustic marking at the track level (only at the album level), lack of resources. |

### 1.2.4. Mathematical and algorithmic apparatus of the engine

The engine replaces naive lexical heuristics with vector matrix analysis and statistical hypothesis testing:

```
[Rough review] ──► [Module 1: UTF-8 Deduplication and Normalization]
                           │
                           ▼
[ Cleaned up text ] ──► [ Module 2: Heuristic Stratification (High/Med/Low Signal) ]
                           │
                           ▼
[ Vector u_i ∈ ℝ³⁸⁴ ] ──► [ Module 4: BLAS Dot-Product by anchor matrix V^T ]
                           │ (τ = 0.40, marginal window Δ = 0.08)
                           ▼
[ Aspect tags + flags ] ─► [ Module 5: Calculating Z-score anomalies (CRITICAL_SPIKE) ]
                           │
                           ▼
[ Analytical slice ] ─► [ Module 6: Calculation of Product Health Score (0 - 100) ]

```

1. **Vector scoring through semantic anchors (Sentence Transformers & BLAS):**
Each review $i$ is encoded with a dense vector $\mathbf{u}_i \in \mathbb{R}^{384}$ using the `all-MiniLM-L6-v2` model with $L_2$-normalization:



$$\|\mathbf{u}_i\|_2 = 1, \quad \|\mathbf{v}_j\|_2 = 1$$



The cosine similarity matrix is ​​calculated via BLAS block multiplication:



$$\mathbf{S} = \mathbf{U} \cdot \mathbf{V}^T, \quad S_{ij} \in [-1, 1]$$



The assignment of the primary microaspect $k^*$ is performed using the hard threshold $\tau = 0.40$:



$$k^* = \arg\max_{k \in \mathcal{A}} \text{Score}_k(i) \quad \text{where} \quad \text{Score}_{k^*}(i) \ge 0.40$$



Secondary aspects $\mathcal{A}_{\text{sec}}(i)$ are captured in the margin window $\Delta = 0.08$:



$$\mathcal{A}_{\text{sec}}(i) = \{ k \in \mathcal{A} \setminus \{k^*\} \mid \text{Score}_k(i) \ge \tau \land \text{Score}_k(i) \ge \text{Score}_{k^*}(i) - 0.08 \}$$


2. **Statistical detection of anomalies and regressions (Z-Score Spikes):**
For each time window or application version, the normal approximation of the difference in the proportion of occurrence of a defect or aspect relative to the historical baseline is checked:



$$Z = \frac{p_{\text{slice}} - p_{\text{baseline}}}{\sqrt{\frac{p_{\text{baseline}}(1 - p_{\text{baseline}})}{N_{\text{slice}}}}}$$



The event is marked with the `CRITICAL_SPIKE` flag if the following conditions are met:



$$Z \ge 2.5 \quad (p < 0.0062), \quad \text{Surge Ratio} = \frac{p_{\text{slice}}}{p_{\text{baseline}}} \ge 1.5\times, \quad C_{\text{slice}} \ge 5$$


3. **Product Health Score:**
The scalar metric $H \in [0, 100]$ aggregates satisfaction, bugs, churn risks and anomalies:



$$H = \max\left(0, \; \min\left(100, \; \left[\frac{\bar{s} - 1.0}{4.0} \times 100\right] - (r_{\text{churn}} \times 50) - (r_{\text{bug}} \times 30) - \min(2k, 15)\right)\right)$$



where $\bar{s}$ is the average star score (1–5), $r_{\text{churn}} \in [0, 1]$ and $r_{\text{bug}} \in [0, 1]$ are the relative shares of churn signals and bug reports (proportions from 0.0 to 1.0, not percentages), $k$ is the number of critical spikes. The scale grades the product from `Healthy` (80–100) to `Critical/Emergency` (0–39.9).


4. **Signal-Based Downsampling:**
When comparing heterogeneous arrays (500k SoundCloud versus 100k YouTube Music), accidental cutting off of meaningful texts in favor of interjections is eliminated. Deterministic sorting is carried out by the ordinal rank of lexical density (`high` $\to$ `medium` $\to$ `low`), ensuring 100% preservation of detailed feedback when balancing samples.

## 1.3. Executive Summary of the Better Spotify Project

### 1.3.1. Context, background and systemic crisis of streaming

Music streaming has reached a structural impasse in its business model and user experience. The transition from the paradigm of owning a media library to renting cloud access (*From Owning to Renting*) has made the audience dependent on closed recommendation algorithms (*Black-Box Algorithms*).

However, optimizing engagement metrics in modern DSPs (Spotify, YouTube Music, Apple Music) has led to a paradox:

1. **Catalogue Imbalance:** Of the 253 million commercial tracks, over 120.5 million (47.6%) have between zero and ten plays in a given year (Luminate, 2025). Recommendation systems concentrate 80% of all plays on just 15% of the catalog (Baumann & Mendler-Dünner, 2024), creating a vicious cycle of super-popular tracks and pushing independent creators into the invisible zone.


2. **Semantic isolation:** Autoplay algorithms are trained on superficial retention signals (skips, stream duration). This forces platforms to recommend safe, easily portable content. According to *Scientific Reports* (2024), algorithmic novelty becomes semantically locked within a single cluster, causing an echo chamber phenomenon (Spotify Ideas, 3,396 votes) and destroying the research diversity that Spotify's own research (Anderson et al., 2020) has shown is a key factor in retaining paying users.


3. **Health Metrics Crisis:** Analysis of almost 1.3 million reviews using our analytical engine revealed the emergency state of the industry: Spotify’s Product Health Score is a critical 24.5 out of 100 (with an overall Churn Rate of 24.31% and Bug Rate of 5.13%), while YouTube Music and Apple Music dropped to 0.0/100 (churn 48.86% and 35.88%, bug rate 22.75%, respectively). The main drivers of churn are the loss of control over playback (`user_control_vs_automation` - 47.7% Churn in Spotify) and system degradation of working with local files (`local_files_and_ownership` - 62.7% Churn in Spotify, 65.7% in YTM).

## 2.1. Industrial Context: The Time-Spent Trap

The modern commercial music streaming (DSP) ecosystem operates in a fundamental conflict between the interests of the end user and the business model of the monopoly platform.

The economics of large platforms (primarily Spotify) are based on maximizing the metric of total time spent in the service (*Time Spent / Session Duration*) and maintaining attention within the advertising or subscription circuit:

* On the free plan (AVOD), revenue is directly proportional to the number of advertising inserts listened to, the frequency of which is tied to the duration of the listening session.


* On a paid tariff (SVOD), the engagement metric serves as an indirect barrier to outflow, however, royalties are distributed according to the so-called *Streamshare* system (proportional to the total share of listening from the total pool), where the key beneficiaries are the majors (Universal, Sony, Warner), which control the vast majority of catalog traffic.

```
┌────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│             THE ECONOMIC MOTIVATION PARADOX OF STREAMING PLATFORMS                     │
├────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┤
│  LISTENER INTEREST:                                                                    │
│  Minimum friction ──► Surgically precise search ──► Instant acoustic resonance         │
│                                                                                        │
│  DSP INTEREST (THE TIME-SPENT TRAP):                                                   │
│  Opaque autoplay ──► Background wandering ──► Maximizing audio ad impressions          │
│  (A tool that quickly finds the target track is unprofitable for the DSP!)[cite: 1]    │
└────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┘
```

From this economic structure emerges a key paradox of the industry, documented in an academic report on streaming: **“A tool that gets a listener to a good track faster is not obviously in the platform’s interest.”*).

If the user quickly finds the music they want and purposefully fulfills their aesthetic need, the session becomes short and focused. In contrast, endless semi-passive wandering through algorithmic radio generates hours of uninterrupted streaming, maximizing advertising inventory.

This is why recommendation systems are optimized not for *depth of aesthetic satisfaction* or *exploratory discovery*, but for the so-called **Safe/Tolerable Listening trade-off: autoplay algorithms learn from superficial signals of engagement (last-listening and lack of immediate skips), systematically favoring neutral, predictable pop songs that do not create a strong desire in the listener to interrupt the background stream. The opacity (“black box”) of recommendations becomes not a technical inevitability, but a conscious corporate policy of retention.

## 2.2. The Anatomy of a Control Deficit: Open Patents vs. Closed Interfaces (The Black-Box Dilemma)

### 2.2.1. US10089578B2 patent precedent and Web API shutdown in November 2024

The widely held claim among streaming platforms that “music recommendations are too complex for direct user control” is belied by the market leaders' own patent and engineering history.

Spotify's patent documentation directly proves that the platform is capable of automatically calculating detailed acoustic descriptors for each track in the catalog. Specifically, **US10089578B2** (Claim 1) specifies the calculation of seven objective acoustic attributes at the track level:

1. *Acousticness* (degree of sound acousticity);
2. *Danceability* (danceability/rhythmic stability);
3. *Energy* (energy/perceptual intensity and loudness);
4. *Instrumentalness* (instrumentality / lack of vocals);
5. *Liveness* (the effect of live presence);
6. *Valence* (musical valence / positivity and emotional coloring);
7. *Tempo* (tempo in BPM).



```

│                        CHRONOLOGY OF CLOSURE OF CONTROL IN SPOTIFY                               │
├──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┤
│ STAGE 1 (2014-2023): OPEN API AND TRACK ATTRIBUTES                                               │
│ • Spotify Web API publishes an Audio Features endpoint for each track[cite: 11].                 │
│ • Recommendations endpoint accepts seed_tracks + target attribute values                         │
│   (for example: target_energy = 0.8, target_valence = 0.2)[cite: 11].                            │
├──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┤
│ STAGE 2 (NOVEMBER 2024): SYSTEM DATA LOCKDOWN (API SHUTDOWN)                                     │
│ • Spotify blocks access to Audio Features for new applications under the pretext                 │
│   "security policy updates"[cite: 11].                                                           │
│ • Developers protest: data isolation for training closed internal AI models[cite: 11].           │

│ • Opaque text prompts (AI DJ, AI Playlists)[cite: 2] are imposed on the user.                    │

```

Moreover, for many years, the official public `Recommendations` endpoint in the Spotify Web API allowed programmatic queueing based on a combination of seed_tracks and precise target attribute ranges (for example, the request: *"put tracks similar to track X, but with target_energy = 0.3 and target_instrumentalness = 0.9"*). **The parametric recommendation engine was already fully implemented and functioning in the Spotify infrastructure**.

However, in **November 2024, Spotify officially removed access to Audio Features for all new external applications**. The official reason was given as “security concerns”, but in the professional development community this decision was seen as data conservation to protect the platform’s proprietary AI models and prevent the emergence of alternative, more efficient listening interfaces.

While regional competitors (Deezer with its *Flow Tuner* mode, Yandex Music with “My Wave” sliders, VK Music with vibe switches) partially open up direct control of flow characteristics for the listener, Spotify has demonstratively moved in the opposite direction - towards complete isolation of the user from algorithmic settings.

### 2.2.2. Cognitive-linguistic barrier to verbalization (The Input Friction)

In parallel with the closure of parametric APIs, streaming giants began to promote the concept of generative search through text and voice prompts (Spotify AI DJ, chat playlists). This has created a critical cognitive impasse in UX: **barrier to verbalization of musical need**.

An academic study by Park & ​​Arthur (2026) demonstrated a fundamental gap between **how a person requests music in words** and **how they perceptually experience and appreciate it**:

* When making a verbal request, the user operates with rough external labels: names of genres (“indie rock”), historical eras (“eighties”) or abstract narratives (“music for a night drive”).


* However, true emotional resonance is determined by purely internal musical-theoretical and acoustic elements: harmonic sequences, mode changes (minor/major), timbre of analog synths, drum syncopation patterns, sound wall density and dynamic range.



```

├────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┤

└────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┘
```

An attempt to force the user to formulate a request in words runs into insurmountable obstacles:

1. **Unpredictability of language models:** Tools based on prompts respond chaotically to subjective descriptions (TechRadar, 2026), regularly producing clichéd selections.


2. **A priori specification requirement:** The platform requires the listener to know exactly and name what he wants *before the first note plays*. If a person is tired of routine, is in search or experiences emotional burnout, he is not able to verbalize the request.


3. **Failure of the classical search model:** Back in 1989, the classic of information retrieval theory Marcia Bates (*Marcia Bates, Berrypicking Model*) proved that in exploratory search the static model “formulated a single query $\to$ received the final list” does not work. A person’s need evolves *directly in the process of becoming familiar with the material*: each fragment heard corrects the trajectory of movement. Traditional DSP interfaces, which require a genre to be fixed at the input, completely deny the dynamic nature of human taste.

## 2.3. The Dark Catalog Problem

### 2.3.1. Catalog asymmetry and the Luminate distribution law

In public discourse, streaming platforms are positioned as “the great democratizers of culture,” giving listeners access to all of humanity’s recorded music (“a library of 100+ million tracks at their fingertips”*). However, real data reveals a catastrophic catalog asymmetry.

According to an independent study of the music market by the analytical agency **Luminate (2025)**:

* Of the **253 million tracks** uploaded to streaming platforms worldwide, **120.5 million tracks (47.6%) received between 0 and 10 plays** during the year under review (Luminate, 2025)!


* Almost half of all recorded music is the so-called **Dark Catalog** - digital ballast that is physically stored on servers, but excluded from the user's attention stream.



```
┌────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│            GLOBAL STREAMING CATALOG DISTRIBUTION (LUMINATE 2025)                       │
├────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┤
│  [■■■■■■■■■■■■■■■■■■■■■■■■■■■■■■■■■■■■■■■■■■■■■■■■■■] 253 MILLION TRACKS               │
│                                                                                        │
│  ████████████████████████▌ (47.6%)  ──►  120.5 MILLION TRACKS: “DARK CATALOG”          │
│                                           (0 to 10 streams per reporting year)[cite: 1]│
│                                                                                        │
│  ████████ (15% of tracks)           ──►  80% OF ALL PLATFORM STREAMS                   │
│                                           (Baumann & Mendler-Dünner, 2024)[cite: 2]    │
└────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┘
```

A study by Baumann & Mendler-Dünner (2024) confirms the mathematical nature of this concentration: modern recommendation algorithms accumulate **80% of total user traffic on just 15% of the directory**.

The reason lies in the architecture of collaborative filtering: in order for the algorithm to recommend a track, it must already have accumulated a matrix of user interactions (listenings, additions, likes). Tracks that are in a “cold start” (niche independent releases, local scenes, experiments) do not have this history. They find themselves in a vicious circle: *no one listens to them because the algorithm doesn’t recommend them, and the algorithm doesn’t recommend them because no one listens to them*.

---

### 2.3.2. Algorithmic pressure and deformation of independent creativity

As a result of the catalog collapse, the costs are borne not by the platform, but by independent musicians and rights holders of small catalogs:

* A study by the UK government agency **CDEI and DCMS (2023)** revealed an unprecedented scale of distortion of the creative process under the dictates of recommender systems:


* **53.5% of musicians** were forced to change the strategy and release schedule to meet the requirements of the algorithms (transition from concept albums to frequent short singles);


* **16.7% of artists** admitted that they **directly change the structure of the music itself** (removing long instrumental intros, artificially shortening the track to 2 minutes, putting a hook in the first 5 seconds to avoid a skip, which kills the track in the eyes of the system);


* **89.2% of content creators** openly report fear of algorithmic bias (*prioritisation bias*) from streaming monopolies.




* At the same time, there is an information vacuum regarding recommendations: only **18% of listeners** and less than **17% of artists** say they understand the principles of how recommendations work, while **61% of listeners** and **83% of musicians** demand transparency and direct disclosure of the reasons for the formation of selections.



---

### 2.3.3. Lack of open track-by-track metadata

An attempt to solve the catalog problem by third-party forces runs into a systemic lack of open labeled data.

Analysis of open music knowledge bases (MusicBrainz, Discogs, Last.fm) shows that they aggregate information about genres, moods and tags **mainly at the level of releases and albums as a whole**. At the level of individual compositions, detailed acoustic and stylistic markings are not publicly available.

Consequently, an independent music product cannot be effectively integrated into search engines without creating its own independent objective acoustic analysis pipeline (DSP/MIR)**.

## 3.2. Academic foundation and theoretical framework (Literature Grounding)

The project's concept is based on fundamental peer-reviewed research in the fields of information retrieval, sociology of cultural consumption, Music Information Retrieval (MIR), and Human-Computer Interaction (HCI).

### 1. Theory of Search Behavior: Marcia Bates (1989) - Berrypicking Model

* **The essence of the study:** The classic work of Marcia Bates refutes the static model of information retrieval (*Classic Information Retrieval*), where the user forms a single request, receives an array of documents and passively studies it. Bates introduces the Berrypicking model: the information need is not static - it continuously evolves and changes as a person encounters new information at each step of the search.


* **Role in the project:** Justification of the inconsistency of the *“Seed-and-Wait”* paradigm (pick one track or genre and wait to see what the radio will give). The request at the end of listening is radically different from the request at the start. This is the theoretical basis for our **dynamic flow control via SongDNA while the track is playing**.



### 2. Large-scale Spotify study: Anderson et al. (WWW 2020)

* **The essence of the study:** Analysis of the behavior of over **100 million Spotify users** and about 70 billion streams, complemented by a controlled experiment on 540,000 users.


* **Key Takeaways:**
1. *“User-driven listening behavior is almost always more diverse than algorithmically-driven”*).


2. Algorithmic recommendations are associated with a decrease in consumption diversity. Users, whose diversity of tastes grew throughout the year, achieved this **by shifting towards their own library and moving away from programmed platform content**.


3. Generalists listeners with high diversity are **25 percentage points less likely to churn** and **35 percentage points more likely to convert to a paid subscription**.




* **Role in the project:** Business case for investors and the jury: returning diversity and control over the media library to the user is a direct tool for saving the paying base from outflow.



### 3. Deezer Study: Scientific Reports (2024) - Reframing the Filter Bubble

* **The essence of the study:** Measurement of 23.9 million listening events of ~50,000 Deezer users in 2.2 million sessions.


* **Key conclusions:** The authors mathematically measured the redundancy coefficient:
* Algorithms: **0.189**;
* Editorial selections: **0.286**;
* Custom playlists: **0.335**;
*Search queries: **0.475**.
A paradoxical conclusion: algorithms formally produce more unique artists than organics, but **this novelty is “semantically confined” within a narrow stylistic cluster**. Intersession analysis has proven that over time, algorithmic sessions inevitably concentrate and collapse.




* **Role in the project:** Justification of the need for the “bubble burst” interface tool - the `Exclude Tags` button and the *Serendipity Jump* mode.



### 4. Psychology of perception: Chmiel & Schubert (2017) and Park & ​​Arthur (2026)

* **The essence of the research:** Music perception follows an inverted $U$-shaped curve (*Inverted-U Curve*): sympathy for a track grows with recognition, reaches a peak, and then plummets due to sensory fatigue from overexposure (*Overexposure Fatigue*). The search for something new is caused not by an abstract craving for novelty, but by fatigue from the boring.


* **Role in the project:** Autoplay tools ignore the accumulation of fatigue and play familiar tracks ad nauseum. Our *Discovery Dial* selector takes into account the fatigue phase and provides a controlled way out of it.



### 5. Evidence of non-stress control: Jin et al. (ContextPlay, UMAP 2019)

* **The essence of the study:** A controlled experiment to evaluate recommendation management interfaces with a control group (No-control baseline).


* **Key Findings:** Direct control of contextual characteristics significantly increases perceived recommendation quality ($p < 0.01$). At the same time, it has been proven: **control does not have a statistically significant effect on the cognitive load of users** (“The control setting has no significant effect on perceived diversity and cognitive load”*). The most frequently changed parameter was Mood, while only a quarter of participants changed genres.


* **Role in the project:** Reinforced concrete parrying of the commission’s counterargument that “the user is lazy and any settings will overload him.”



### 6. Ergonomics of controls: Jin et al. (UMAP 2018)

* **Gist of the Study:** Comparison of numerical sliders with radar charts in music research. Sliders significantly lost to the radar chart in terms of open rates (4.2 vs. 4.72, $p = 0.009$). Users with low expertise were confused by the number scales and abandoned the search more often.


* **Role in the project:** Rationale for abandoning abstract numerical percentages in favor of clickable SongDNA tags and clear three-position selectors.



### 7. CDEI & DCMS UK Government Report (2023) / Prof. David Hesmondhalgh

* **The essence of the study:** A comprehensive analysis of the impact of algorithms on the UK music industry (Deltapoll survey of ~4,120 adults, artist research).


* **Key findings:** 53.5% of musicians changed their release strategy, 16.7% changed the very structure of tracks to suit algorithms, 89.2% are afraid of platform bias. Only 18% of listeners understand the recommendations, and 61% demand transparency (functions *“Why am I seeing this recommendation”*).


* **Role in the project:** Implementation of the mandatory Explainable UI block (*“Selected based on characteristics...”*) and protection of artists through transparent ranking rules.



### 8. Study of the position of musicians: ISMIR (2023)

* **The essence of the study:** Interviews with 14 independent artists and a survey of 34 industry professionals about control and transparency.


* **Key Takeaways:** Musicians independently proposed giving listeners direct control through tags and prompts, linking the listener's right to filter with copyright fairness for the small stage.


* **Role in the project:** Confirmation that our SongDNA interface is supported by the content authors themselves.



### 9. Sociology of interaction with AI: Víctor Ávila Torres & David Beer (2025)

* **The essence of the study:** Studying the daily routines of managing algorithms and forming trust (*Algorithm Trust and Objective Setting*).


* **Key takeaways:** Listeners actively interfere with the algorithms, trying to make them work for themselves. Trust is only built when the platform allows the user to set their own session goals.



### 10. Track-by-track coherence: EPJ Data Science (2025)

* **The essence of the research:** Development of mathematical metrics for the coherence of human playlists. The authors proved that human selection is based on subtle acoustic connections that are lost in algorithms due to lack of access to audio features.

During the product design process, the Better Spotify project went through a series of internal discussions and cutting off dead-end product concepts identified early in the team's work.

### 4.1.1. The Pitfall of “A New Spotify from Scratch” (The Full-Stack / UGC Fallacy)

The team’s initial hypothesis envisaged the creation of a classic independent full-cycle streaming service with open uploading of user content following the model of the early VK Music (“upload everything without censorship, delete only based on complaints from copyright holders”).

The crash test revealed the non-viability of this concept based on three criteria:

1. **Capex Barrier:** Licensing the catalog of the Big Three (Universal, Sony, Warner) requires tens of millions of dollars in upfront non-refundable deposits (Minimum Guarantees), which is impossible for an independent startup.
2. **Legal Impasse (Safe Harbor Elimination):** With the adoption of Article 17 of the EU Copyright Directive and the tightening of App Store policies (Guideline 5.2), the platform organizing algorithmic playback of UGC audio is deprived of information intermediary immunity and bears direct subsidiary liability for pirated copies from the moment they are downloaded. An attempt to launch unmoderated streaming leads to instant judicial blocking and removal from marketplaces.
3. **Price positioning:** An attempt to compete head-on with Spotify on its own field (paid subscription to 100 million of the same pop tracks) lacks differentiation: users have no motivation to migrate to a less stable clone of a well-known service.



---

### 4.1.2. The Dependency Trap

The second hypothesis (proposed by Bogdan at the early stage of the formation of Proposal: “A design proposal for a Music Discovery tool”) involved abandoning our own player and creating a lightweight UI plugin or web add-on that manages the playback queue through the official Spotify Web API.

This concept was rejected during the dialectical audit for strategic reasons:

1. **Precedent for the November 2024 lockdown (API Vulnerability):**
In November 2024, Spotify blocked access to the `Audio Features` endpoint for new external applications. The platform has clearly demonstrated its willingness to destroy any third-party developer ecosystem that attempts to improve or modify the user experience outside of the official client. Building a product as an add-on over the API of a direct competitor is a fatal architectural dependency (Platform Risk).


2. **Context of mobile consumption:**
Over 85% of music listening occurs on smartphones (with headphones, while driving, while jogging, in the subway). Browser extensions for desktop computers do not cover mobile listening scenarios, turning the product into a highly specialized desktop utility for a narrow group of enthusiasts.



---

### 4.1.3. The principle of 100% routine: Why the “second application” is doomed to death

Key product argument recorded in the team discussion: **“People don’t listen to music in two applications at the same time”**.

* The mental model of the listener is monogamous: in the smartphone operating system there is exactly one basic audio player, which occupies a widget slot in the notification shade and on the lock screen.


* If you offer a user a product that *only* finds obscure indie music, but doesn't let you listen to their usual everyday tracks, the user will open it once a month out of curiosity, but will continue to pay for Spotify.


* **Transition rule:** To force the user to transfer his audio library, the product must close **100% of his routine daily scenarios** (playing favorite music, background mode, working with playlists, offline) and **on top of this hygienic minimum** provide unique functionality for deep search and control of recommendations.

### 4.2.1. Engine 1: Cloud Discovery Engine

* **Cold Start Catalog (Day 1):** Legal pool of over **750,000 studio tracks** available under non-commercial open licenses:
* *Jamendo Music API:* Over 600,000 tracks from independent creators from around the world, distributed under Creative Commons licenses (CC BY, CC BY-SA, CC BY-NC). Jamendo's official open API allows you to legally integrate audio streams into third-party applications.
* *Free Music Archive (FMA) & Netlabels:* Over 150,000 avant-garde, electronic, instrumental and indie releases curated by independent radio stations (WFMU) and netlabels.


* **Creator Portal:** A constantly updated catalog of new independent authors who upload their master recordings directly through our portal with a free distribution license.
* **Pre-indexing:** Each cloud catalog track is pre-digitized by a batch server pipeline and provided with a persistent SongDNA feature vector (BPM, harmony, mode, spectral energy, objective tags).



---

### 4.2.2. Engine 2: Local Storage Bridge

* **Absolute autonomy of the media library:** The application includes a high-performance native player of local audio files, scanning the permanent memory of the smartphone and SD card (FLAC, ALAC, WAV, MP3, AAC, OGG formats).


* **Eliminating an industry blind spot:**
According to our Data Engine, the `local_files_and_ownership` aspect exhibits a **catastrophic Churn Rate (62.7% on Spotify and 65.7% on YouTube Music)**. In Spotify, local files fall off when updating, don't sync, and are locked in a separate, non-indexable tab.


* **First-Class Citizens:** In Better Spotify, a user's local files have the same rights in the interface as cloud streams: they fully participate in the search, are sorted by artist and album, added to general queues, and support instant scrobbling.



---

### 4.2.3. Intellectual bridge SongDNA Bridge (Synthesis of two worlds)

The key innovation of the Dual-Engine is that **local files cease to be an isolated archive and become a catalyst for musical discovery**:

1. **Listening scenario:** The user launches a world-famous cult track from his device (for example, *“Electioneering”* by Radiohead or *“Canned Heat”* Jamiroquai in FLAC format).


2. **Background Edge Analysis:** Built-in lightweight client module analyzes the audio stream (determines 128 BPM tempo, D Minor key, high guitar density, raw analog overdrive).


3. **Cloud request to the bridge:** The client sends a compact numeric feature profile to the server (without transmitting the protected file itself!).


4. **Seamless transition:** The recommended stream brings up the following composition **not a promoted pop hit, but a track by an unknown independent guitar band from the cloud catalog Day 1**, which has an identical harmonic structure and sound energy.


5. **Result:** The user gets access to their favorite global classics while discovering fresh niche talent in one continuous stream of playback.

## 4.5. Security barriers and antifragility of the business model (Strategic Moats & Defensibility)

Before defending at PBL, the project formulates four institutional barriers (Moats) to ensure that the product cannot be easily copied by tech giants:

### 1. The Innovator's Dilemma & Major Label Lock-in

Spotify fails to implement the transparent three-position `Exclude Pop` filters and `Obscurity: Deep Underground` slider.

Spotify's business model is directly tied to the **Discovery Mode** program: majors agree to a reduced royalty rate in exchange for the fact that the Spotify algorithm artificially inflates the rotation of their commercial releases in autoplay and radio. If Spotify gives users a tool to harshly cut out content paid for by labels, the advertising model for monetizing recommendations will collapse, and the majors will break distribution contracts. **Our independence from major catalogs is our main strategic moat**.

### 2. The Subscription Cannibalization Barrier

DSPs have historically sabotaged the quality of support for a user's local files (`local_files`) because their unit economics are based on forcing the listener to pay for cloud access every month. If Spotify makes local play perfect, some of the audience will return to their personal archives and cancel their subscription. Better Spotify is built from the ground up around the synergy of local audio and open cloud, turning the giants' Achilles heel into its core feature.

### 3. Protecting the intellectual property of the algorithm (IP Moat via Decoy Noise)

The client-server split architecture protects our core recommendations:

* The client application acts as a “blind scanner”, extracting an extended spectral profile with injection of false noise parameters (Decoy Features).
* Calibration formula, SongDNA latent space and multimodal clustering weights are private on the server. Competitors cannot extract the model by decompiling the mobile application.

### 4. Legal and infrastructural antifragility (Zero Copyright & Storage Liability)

Due to the fact that the user's personal files are processed locally on the device (Edge MIR Engine), and only a numeric JSON package of descriptors of 40–80 KB in size is transferred to the server, our backend:

* **Does not store a single byte of unlicensed audio** $\to$ zero risks of copyright lawsuits (Zero Copyright Footprint).
* **No costs for storing petabytes of user files (S3/CDN)** $\to$ server unit economics converge even when scaling to millions of active users.

## 5.1. Architectural Decomposition: Search vs. Recommendation Stream

The fundamental interface defect of existing streaming services is the mixture of two diametrically opposed modes of user interaction with the music catalog: **targeted search** and **background streaming**.

In the Better Spotify project, these processes are strictly separated at the level of mental models, types of user intent (User Intent) and mathematical restrictions of the system:

```
┌──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│                        THE DISCOVERY DIAL OPERATIONAL MODES                              │
├──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┤
│ 1. “BUBBLE” MODE (ZERO RISK):                                                            │
│ • Metric threshold: Cosine Similarity >= 0.92.                                           │
│ • Catalog: Strictly trusted tracks from personal library and close artist catalog.       │
│ • Scenario: Listener wants predictable focus or curates a specific playlist[cite: 1, 5]. │
├──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┤
│ 2. “DRIFT” MODE (EXPLORATION DRIFT — RECOMMENDED DEFAULT):                               │
│ • Metric threshold: Cosine Similarity within 0.70 – 0.85.                                │
│ • Mechanics: Preserves key and tempo while introducing acoustic timbre shifts.           │
│ • Scenario: Listener steers session in real-time, discovering indie gems[cite: 5].       │
├──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┤
│ 3. “SERENDIPITY” MODE (WILDCARD / RADICAL SERENDIPITY):                                  │
│ • Metric threshold: Cosine Similarity within 0.40 – 0.60.                                │
│ • Mechanics: Cross-genre bridges (e.g. Atmospheric Black Metal ──► Medieval Folk).       │
│ • Scenario: Breaking out of algorithmic echo chambers into radical new sounds.           │
└──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┘
```

| Design criterion | Search and cataloging module (Search Engine) | Stream module (Recommendation Stream) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Custom Intent** | Active sampling; willingness to invest cognitive resource in filtering. | Background playback (work, driving, sports); minimum interface friction requirement. |
| **Mathematical model** | **Hard Bounds:** cutting off inappropriate objects ($Score = 0$ if $BPM \notin [68, 74]$ or $Genre \ne \text{Indie}$). | **Soft Pull:** probabilistic shift of the latent space centroid towards given tags. |
| **Queue behavior** | Reset current session; generating a new static playlist. | Seamlessly calculate the next track ($t+1$) without stopping the current track ($t$). |
| **Exception Handling** | Strict exception: the `NOT` operator completely removes marked tracks from the output. | Filtering barrier: a relevancy penalty coefficient that reduces the probability of a track being dropped to zero. |

### 5.2.1. Why Concept Art 1.0 solutions were considered vulnerable

An early prototype (Concept Art 1.0) emphasized gesture control and spatial coordinates:

* Dragging the record into 4 genre corners (Graph Mode: Funk, Jazz Rap, Neo-Soul, Experimental Hip-Hop) and a 2D map with axes [Old/New vs Popular/Underground] required continuous visual control and high musical erudition from the listener.


* Study by Jin et al. (UMAP 2018) proved: abstract multi-axis controls cause confusion among users with low and medium expertise, forcing them to reset the session. For the `passive_listener` cohort (129,426 users in the Spotify sample), who listen to music while driving or while working, this interface was unsuitable.

#### Mechanics of SongDNA Tri-State Tag Chips

Below the track metadata, objective tags of the current song, extracted by the SongDNA algorithm (Genre, Instruments, Mood, Vibe), are dynamically displayed:

* **⚪ Neutral / State 0:** The tag is gray. The algorithm takes it into account in the background in accordance with the standard track profile.


* **🟢 Include in attractor (Must Have / State 1 / Tap):** The chip lights up green with a `[+]` icon. The algorithm immediately recalculates the latent vector of the queue: the following recommendations must contain this acoustic or stylistic feature.


* **🔴 Exclude from stream (Exclude / State 2 / Repeated tap or swipe right):** The chip turns red with the `[-]` icon. The algorithm imposes a severe penalty on this attribute, guaranteeing the complete disappearance of tracks of this type from the session (for example: excluding commercial pop vocals `#Pop = Exclude`).


* **Reset (State 3 / Third tap):** Return to neutral state.



#### Combinatorial Tag Stacking Logic

The Art 2.0 concept implements a strict logic for combining features without the need to enter formulas:

* **Tags of the same class (for example, several genres):** Work through logical addition (OR). Selecting `[+ #Indie]` and `[+ #Hip-Hop]` produces a stream containing both indie and hip-hop tracks.


* **Tags of opposite classes (Genre $\times$ Theme/Vibe):** Work through convergence (AND). The combination of `[+ #Hip-Hop]` and `[+ #Melancholic]` brings the output strictly into the realm of melancholic instrumental hip-hop.


* **Exclusion Tags:** Have absolute priority: if `[- #Electronic]` is enabled, any indie/electronic hybrids are immediately cut off.



#### The Discovery Dial

At the bottom of the player there is a compact three-position toggle switch (radial dial or switch), which determines the autopilot strategy:

1. **Harmonic Bubble Mode:**
* *Math:* The search radius in the feature space is narrowed to a minimum ($\epsilon \le 0.15$).


* *Scenario:* Background work, study, meditation. The listener is protected from sudden sound changes; the algorithm maintains the ideal homogeneous vibe of the twin tracks.




2. **Gradual Drift Mode:**
* *Math:* Practical implementation of the *Berrypicking* model by Marcia Bates. Every 3–4 tracks, the algorithm smoothly shifts one of the secondary acoustic descriptors by a small step $\delta$, maintaining the harmonic basis.


* *Scenario:* Natural musical journey: starting with an atmospheric line, the listener after an hour organically finds himself in a cinematic ambient, without experiencing dissonance.




3. **Serendipity Jump Mode:**
* *Math:* The algorithm captures emotional valence and rhythmic energy, but inverts the genre cluster by selecting a track from a diametrically opposed cultural era (for example: the transition from 70s neo-soul to modern alternative trip-hop).


* *Scenario:* Overcoming sensory fatigue from a familiar sound (combat overexposure Chmiel & Schubert).

## 5.6. Semantic Conflict Resolution (Fallback & Soft Attractor Logic)

When designing Boolean control interfaces, there is inevitably a risk of generating mutually exclusive requests from the user (for example, simultaneously selecting `[+ #AcousticFolk]` and `[+ #IndustrialTechno]`, or setting `[BPM: 60-65]` together with `[Energy: Maximum Aggressive]`).

Better Spotify implements a three-level algorithmic deadlock prevention system:

```
[ User activates mutually exclusive tags ]
                        │
                        ▼
       (1. Intermediate Latent Projection)
       Search for hybrid cross-genre nodes in catalog
       (Folk-industrial, acoustic dark ambient with distorted beat)
                        │
         ┌──────────────┴──────────────┐
         ▼                             ▼
   [ Nodes Found ]              [ Density = 0 ]
   Standard queue                      │
   generation                          ▼
                        (2. Relaxation & Fallback)
                        Smooth relaxation of secondary boundaries
                        while preserving dominant acoustic vibe
                                       │
                                       ▼
                        (3. A/B Alternating Queue)
                        Alternating contrasting tracks in queue
                        with Explainable UI transparency cards
```

1. **Projection into latent cross-genre nodes (Latent Space Bridging):**
The recommender module does not operate on flat tables of a relational database, but on a continuous vector space of features. For polar queries, the algorithm searches for rare experimental compositions at the intersection of cultural layers (for example, acoustic neo-folk with heavy industrial synthesizers).
2. **Soft relaxation of restrictions (Constraint Relaxation):**
If the density of the open directory at a given point in space is zero, the system does not crash with the error “Nothing found.” The algorithm automatically weakens the narrowest minor frames (for example, expands the BPM window by $\pm 10$ beats), while maintaining priority genre chips.
3. **Alternating Queue:**
If it is impossible to reconcile the tags, the interface forms a “chessboard” queue of tracks (track A of style is replaced by track of style B), and the Explainable UI line honestly informs the user: *“Mixing polar genres: Folk acoustics alternates with industrial beats”*.

The system maintains 100% fault tolerance, preventing interruption of the music stream during any user experiments.

# SECTION 6. TECHNICAL ARCHITECTURE AND DSP/MIR PIPELINES (ENGINEERING)

## 6.1. System topology: Client-server split (Edge Computing vs. Cloud Backend)

The key engineering challenge when designing a next-generation streaming service is distributing the computing load between the client and the server cluster.

The classic approach of monopolists (Spotify, Apple Music) involves complete centralization: the mobile application acts as a “thin client” (Dummy Client), which only receives a compressed audio stream and sends click telemetry, while all acoustic analysis (MIR - *Music Information Retrieval*) is performed on the platform servers. However, an attempt to transfer this model to the processing of the user's personal local files (`local_files`) leads to infrastructural, financial and legal collapse:

* **Infrastructure deadlock (Ingress Bandwidth):** In the case of a hypothetical naive implementation with raw files transferred to the server (if 50,000 active users download only 5 local tracks per week of 20-50 MB FLAC/MP3 in size), the volume of incoming server traffic would be **from 5 to 12.5 terabytes per week**. Reception, decompression and storage of such arrays would require constant expansion of the server fleet and enormous costs for incoming traffic (Cloud Egress/Ingress).
* **Legal Trap (Copyright & Storage Liability):** As soon as the server accepts and stores in storage (S3/Ceph) a user file with a commercial recording (for example, an album by Radiohead or The Weeknd), the platform legally becomes the host of unlicensed content, falling under direct liability for copyright infringement (DMCA / EU Copyright Directive).



The Better Spotify project resolves this contradiction by introducing a **hybrid client-server split (Edge-Cloud Distributed Pipeline)**:


### 6.2.2. Mathematical specification of extractable SongDNA descriptors

The client module (assembled on the C++ core of the Essentia library and compiled for mobile platforms via WebAssembly / NDK) extracts a feature vector along three orthogonal planes of music theory and acoustics:

#### 1. Temporal & Rhythm Metrics

* **BPM (Beats Per Minute) & Tempo Grid:** Tempo is calculated through the attack detection function (*Onset Detection Function*), logarithmic filtering of spectral fluctuations and autocorrelation of the novelty envelope (*Novelty Curve*).


* **Beat Strength:** The ratio of the spectral energy on strong metrical beats to the total energy of the beat (determines the clarity of the rhythmic grid: marching rhythm vs. floating ambient free-time).
* **Syncopation Index:** Mathematical assessment of the shift in emphasis from strong to weak beats (critical for funk, jazz and breakbeat).

#### 2. Harmonic & Pitch Chroma

* **HPCP (Harmonic Pitch Class Profiles / 12-gray chromatogram):**
The audio signal is divided into frames using a windowed Fourier transform or Constant-Q Transform (CQT). The distribution of harmonic energy over 12 semitones of the octave ($C, C\sharp, D \dots B$) is calculated:



$$\mathbf{h} = [h_0, h_1, \dots, h_{11}]^T, \quad \sum_{i=0}^{11} h_i = 1$$


* **Key & Mode Identification:**
The track chromogram is compared through a scalar product with the reference Krumhansl-Schmuckler Key Profiles and Temperley profiles. The tonic (for example, $B$) and modal scale (*Natural Minor, Harmonic Minor, Dorian, Major*) are determined.


* **Harmonic Complexity & Inharmonicity:**
* *Inharmonicity:* Measures the deviation of overtones from the ideal harmonic series of integers ($f_n \ne n \cdot f_0$). A high rate is typical for overdriven guitars (distortion), bells and industrial sounds; low - for academic piano and clean synthesizers.
* *Chroma Centroid Variance:* Variance of chord degree changes. Distinguishes tracks with 1–2 power chords (drones, sludge, black metal) from polyphonic jazz modulations.





#### 3. Spectral and timbre descriptors (Timbral & Spectral Shape)

* **Spectral Centroid:**
The “center of gravity” of the frequency spectrum, which correlates with the subjective perception of sound brightness:

$$f_c = \frac{\sum_{k=0}^{N-1} f(k) \cdot |X(k)|}{\sum_{k=0}^{N-1} |X(k)|}$$



Low $f_c$ corresponds to a dull, dark, sub-bass sound; high - bright, sharp, full of cymbals and high-pitched vocals.
* **Spectral Flatness:**
The ratio of the geometric mean spectrum to the arithmetic one. Separates tonal harmonic sound (musical instruments) from white/pink noise (noise, rain sounds, wet overdrive).
* **Spectral Rolloff:**
The frequency below which 85% of the total spectral energy of the composition is concentrated.
* **Tristimulus (Three-component timbral balance):**
The acoustic equivalent of the RGB color model: measures the relative weight of the first fundamental harmonic ($T_1$), the second to fourth harmonics ($T_2$) and all higher overtones ($T_3$), describing the texture of the timbre.
* **MFCC (Mel-Frequency Cepstral Coefficients):**
The first 13 mel-cepstral coefficients, which fix the overall timbre envelope regardless of the pitch of the fundamental tone (the basis for filtering vocal textures and separating acoustic and synthetic instruments).

## 7.3. Phase 2: Deterministic Harmonic Synthesis (Music Theory vs. Deep Learning)

### 7.3.1. Why Symbolic Transformers Fail

During the technical design process, the team thoroughly explored the possibility of using open lightweight MIDI generation neural networks (such as *Pop Music Transformer*, *MidiCaps* or models based on the *Lakh MIDI* dataset).

Their use was unanimously recognized as an architectural error for three critical reasons:

1. **Unremovable bias of training datasets (Dataset Bias):**
99% of open source symbolic neural networks are trained on arrays of popular music from the 2000s, piano transcriptions of pop hits, and standard commercial EDM. If you feed such a model input with references of black metal, synthwave, dark ambient or modal jazz, the model inevitably “slides” into banal pop cadences ($I - V - vi - IV$) and major seventh chords, completely destroying the authentic dark atmosphere of the original source.
2. **Hallucinations of dissonance and lack of control of form:**
The neural network generates note tokens stochastically (probabilistically). She lacks a rigid knowledge of the laws of acoustics: she is capable of abruptly ending a phrase on a dissonant tritone, missing a fatal false interval in the middle of a bar, or forgetting to resolve the harmonic tension into the tonic at the end of a 16-bar structure.
3. **Black Box vs. PBL Academic Rigor:**
For exam boards and academic experts, the statement “we took a pre-trained neural network and pressed a button” has zero scientific value. On the contrary, the strict deterministic model of music theory proves the fundamental engineering maturity of the team.

---

### 7.3.2. Conceptual outline of synthesis based on the rules of music theory

Instead of stochastic neural networks, Phase 2 is implemented through a **deterministic pipeline of functional harmony and music theory** (based on the open academic library `music21`):

## 10.4. Engineering Computing Unit (Edge Computing, Performance & IP)

For weak chipsets or when the battery is low, the audio stream is resampled in hardware to mono at a frequency of 22,050 Hz. * **Compiled kernel in C++ / WebAssembly:**

* **Rate Limiting:**
The free plan has a balancing quota: **no more than 5 analyzes of local tracks per week**. This physically eliminates continuous overheating of the device and background battery discharge.
