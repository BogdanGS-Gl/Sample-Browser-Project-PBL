H1. The ability to search segments in songs through acoustic descriptions will substantially reduce the time required to find and evaluate candidate samples.
H2. The ease-of-use and time efficiency of our solution will attract producers who've never/rarely tried to sample before.
H3. The ability to playback segments from song recommendations makes text-based keyword queries more efficient in browsing music for the purpose of finding samples
H4. Manual contribution is a reliable way of creating a catalog of categorized music segments, as long as users are rewarded and contributing is fast and intuitive.
H5. Small-time producers prefer unlicensed samples rather than pre-curated licensed libraries.


1. BK: How would you describe the genre of your music, and what inspires your production style?
2. BK: When making a song, do you rely on instruments you own or use digital audio?
3. BK: What part of music production is the easiest for you and why? H1: How long does the whole process take for you?
4. BK: Do you release music under a production alias or collaborate professionally with other artists? Do you make music for money, personal enjoyment, or both? 
5. Screening: Are you familiar with the process of sampling? Have you tried sampling in your songs before, how many samples did you use on the last track, and why did you choose them?
	H2: If not: Why? Is it because of lack of time, difficulty of the process, art block, or something else?
	H2: What would make you try sampling?

6. H5: Do you use pre-curated sample libraries, or do you prefer sampling directly from original songs? 
	H5: If using one over the other: Is it more convenient for you, less costly, or for some other reason? 
	H5: Do you use paid methods to find samples or free alternatives? Can you list off which you use most often? 
	(If answered "paid methods") H5: Have you ever had problems with using licensed samples when producing music?
7. H1: How much time do you dedicate towards finding samples, and when do you consider you've spent enough?

8. H1: What was the last sound you searched for, and how did you describe it when searching for it online?
	H3: How often do you find a good sample from a single search query? How many samples did you last preview before you found one you did use?

9. 	H3: Does it make a significant difference whether or not you can hear a sample before downloading it? 
H3: Do you download sample libraries in mass and preview them in your DAW, or do you audition them before downloading?

10. H4: Do you have a personal library where you store your found samples? How do you categorize it? Do you (prefer to) store it locally or online?
	H4: If you could tag samples while crate-digging and save them to an online library, would you be willing to share those saved song sections publicly, or keep it purely private? Would receiving credit for your tags change that decision?

Changes: Reworded many of the questions, cut out about 3 of them and combined them into previous questions. Also tagged each question by its purpose,
whether it answers a hypothesis, gives background info, or serves as a screening question. 
Potential flaws: Some questions might only give guess answers or they're "Anyone would answer yes" type questions, but I can't be objective on that verdict.
Some questions might also be "Would {Solution} make your life easier?" which also give redundant answers. The main thing to watch out for is what variety of answers can
be received from the questions, which ones are closed yes/no questions and which ones are open(invite descriptive answers).

Questions i cut include "Walk me through your sampling process", because, although good in theory, I believe answers would be lengthy, mainly filler(because we only care about the initial step in production), and music production is non-linear, so it varies from song to song in artistic intent(instrument you compose on changes, sample sources change, challenges, beliefs, goals change etc.)

I didn't change most questions because they were bad before, but I was thinking what kind of questions I would know how to answer as a producer. I didn't want questions
to sound too complex or demanding, so I attempted to reword them to be a bit more specific.
